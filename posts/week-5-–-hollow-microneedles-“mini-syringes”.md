## **What are hollow microneedles?**

Hollow microneedles (HMNs) take a fundamentally different approach from solid, coated, or dissolving microneedles.

Instead of relying on diffusion or a dissolving matrix, HMNs use a central lumen to actively deliver liquid formulations into the skin. They are similar to mini‑hypodermic needles, with lumens connecting to the centre to deliver liquid formulations into the dermis.

This design enables precise control of dosage and delivery rates, making HMNs a promising alternative to conventional injections.

## **How hollow microneedles are used**

Hollow microneedles are particularly well suited for:

* **High‑dose or viscous formulations:** Larger drug volumes can be delivered compared to coated or dissolving MNs.
* **Biologics and macromolecules:** Proteins, monoclonal antibodies, chemotherapeutics, and other large molecules can be delivered directly into the dermis.
* **Controlled or continuous infusion:** Flow rate, pressure, and delivery rate can be modulated for bolus, continuous, or pulsatile dosing.
* **Diagnostics and monitoring:** Interstitial fluid can be extracted via capillary action for sampling.

## **Advantages of hollow microneedles**

Hollow microneedles offer several advantages over other microneedle types and conventional delivery methods:

* **Higher dose capacity.** Can accommodate larger drug volumes than coated or dissolving MNs, suitable for high‑dose or viscous formulations.
* **Controlled infusion.** Flow rate, pressure, and delivery rate can be modulated for bolus, continuous, or pulsatile dosing.
* **Precise dosing.** Actuators can regulate drug delivery rate per unit time, optimising therapeutic efficacy while minimising tissue damage.
* **Suitable for biologics and macromolecules.** Can deliver proteins, monoclonal antibodies, chemotherapeutics, and other large molecules directly into the dermis.
* **Dual use.** Can also extract interstitial fluid via capillary action for diagnostic sampling.

## **Challenges and limitations**

Despite their advantages, hollow microneedles come with several challenges:pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih+6

* **Clogging and leakage.** The lumen can become blocked by compressed skin tissue or drug particulates, hindering delivery and increasing infection risk.
* **Mechanical fragility.** Hollow MNs are relatively weaker than solid or coated MNs and prone to failure under axial and lateral loads.
* **Complex fabrication and higher cost.** Manufacturing hollow structures is more difficult and expensive than solid or coated designs.
* **Insertion variability.** Actual penetration depth is often lower than nominal length due to skin elasticity, deformation, and insertion force variability.
* **Need for precise flow control.** Applicators must regulate infusion rates carefully to avoid tissue damage or inconsistent dosing.

## **CMC and regulatory considerations**

For CMC teams, hollow microneedles raise several important questions:pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih+3

* **Material selection.** Silicon, glass, stainless steel, or polymers each have different implications for strength, biocompatibility, and cost.
* **Lumen design and flow control.** Needle diameter, lumen length, insertion depth, and skin backpressure all affect infusion rate and must be characterised.
* **Mechanical testing.** Must ensure needles withstand insertion forces without bending, breaking, or lumen collapse.
* **Sterility and biocompatibility.** Critical for preventing infection and ensuring patient safety.
* **Regulatory pathway.** In the US, hollow MNs delivering separately approved formulations are regulated as Class II medical devices (510(k) pathway); in the EU, MDR Rule 14 classifies drug‑device combinations as Class III, requiring consultation with a medicines authority.

## **Why hollow microneedles matter for CMC**

Hollow microneedles offer a compelling option for high‑dose, controlled, or continuous delivery—but their success depends heavily on getting the lumen design, flow control, and mechanical robustness right.

For sponsors, they raise distinct questions around:

* Formulation compatibility with lumen materials and flow dynamics.
* Device design, mechanical testing, and human‑factors engineering.
* Analytical methods to assess flow rate, dose accuracy, and lumen integrity.
* Scale‑up, packaging, and storage conditions that preserve device performance.
* Regulatory strategy for a device‑only or combination‑product pathway.
