## The best candidate must be developable

Candidate selection is often dominated by biological activity, selectivity, potency, and early safety signals. Those factors are essential—but they are not the whole development decision.

A candidate also needs to be practical to develop, manufacture, store, transport, and scale. If supply-chain constraints are considered only after selection, teams may discover that the preferred molecule depends on scarce materials, a single supplier, specialised manufacturing capability, or a process that does not translate easily beyond the laboratory.

Supply-chain de-risking at candidate selection does not mean designing the final commercial network before the lead is chosen. It means identifying constraints early enough to influence the development strategy.

## Risks worth assessing early

A structured early review can consider:

* Availability and qualification status of critical starting materials, excipients, components, and consumables.
* Supplier concentration, geographic exposure, lead times, and minimum order requirements.
* Manufacturability, process complexity, scale-up potential, and reliance on specialised equipment.
* Formulation and stability requirements, including temperature sensitivity and shipping constraints.
* Availability of alternative suppliers, manufacturing sites, materials, or process routes.
* Potential impact of future changes on comparability, control strategy, and regulatory filings.lek+4

Some risks may be acceptable. The important point is that they should be visible, understood, and deliberately managed—not discovered when they are already threatening a clinical milestone.

## Phase-appropriate does not mean supply-blind

Early development does not require every supply-chain decision to be final. It does require a proportionate assessment of the risks most likely to affect candidate progression.

For example, an early review may identify that a candidate:

* Requires a specialised excipient available from only one qualified source.
* Has a formulation that is highly sensitive to temperature or agitation.
* Depends on a low-throughput process that may be difficult to scale.
* Needs a manufacturing platform with limited capacity or long booking timelines.
* Has no obvious alternative material or supplier if the primary source changes.

These findings can inform candidate ranking, process development priorities, supplier engagement, inventory planning, and the timing of backup strategies.

## From candidate choice to resilient development

The most effective approach connects candidate selection with CMC and supply-chain planning from the beginning:

* Screen developability and manufacturability alongside biological performance.
* Map the critical materials and suppliers before they become embedded in the process.
* Identify which risks require early technical work, dual sourcing, safety stock, or alternative process options.
* Maintain a clear record of assumptions, uncertainties, and mitigation decisions.
* Revisit the risk assessment as the molecule, process, formulation, and clinical plan evolve.
